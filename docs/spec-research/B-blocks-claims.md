# Track B — Traditional block library, public-domain sources, naming and copyright: claims

The eleven claims the proposals depend on most. For each: the claim in one sentence, the URL, and the exact supporting sentence in quotation marks. A reviewer will re-open every one. Access date for all: 2026-10-28.

1. **The current US public-domain cutoff is 1930 — works published in 1930 and earlier entered the public domain on 2026-01-01.**
   URL: https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026/
   "On January 1, 2026, thousands of copyrighted works from 1930 enter the US public domain, along with sound recordings from 1925."

2. **The US Copyright Office will not register common geometric shapes standing alone.**
   URL: https://law.resource.org/pub/us/compendium/ch900.html
   "The Copyright Act does not protect common geometric shapes, either in two- dimensional or three-dimensional form."

3. **Familiar symbols and designs are not protected by the Copyright Act, and mere variations on them are likewise unprotected.**
   URL: https://law.resource.org/pub/us/compendium/ch900.html
   "Familiar symbols and designs are not protected by the Copyright Act."

4. **A pattern-book registration does not extend to the individual pattern pieces used to make an article.**
   URL: https://law.resource.org/pub/us/compendium/ch900.html
   "A registration for a pattern book generally does not extend to individual pattern pieces that may be used to create a useful article, such as shapes that may be traced and used to make a sleeve for a dress, because they have an intrinsic utilitarian function."

5. **In a factual compilation, copyright protects only the selection and arrangement, never the underlying facts.**
   URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340
   "A factual compilation is eligible for copyright if it features an original selection or arrangement of facts, but the copyright is limited to the particular selection or arrangement. In no event may copyright extend to the facts themselves."

6. **"Dear Jane" is a live USPTO trademark owned by the Brenda M. Papadakis Living Trust, covering quilt-making instruction.**
   URL: https://www.trademarkia.com/dear-jane-76010949
   "DEAR JANE is a registered trademark (Registration #2423565) owned by BRENDA M. PAPADAKIS LIVING TRUST, a CIBOLO based entity located in TX."

7. **Electric Quilt states that its Dear Jane block drawings are copyrighted material taken from Brenda Papadakis's book.**
   URL: https://support.electricquilt.com/articles/copyright/
   "The block designs in the Dear Jane Add-on for EQ8 (and original Dear Jane software on CD) come from the Dear Jane book by Brenda Papadakis and are copyrighted."

8. **The Ladies Art Company catalogue existed from c.1897 through a 1928 edition, all before the public-domain cutoff.**
   URL: https://quiltindex.org/collections/special-collections/12-91-513/
   "The Ladies Art Company of St. Louis, Missouri was the earliest quilt pattern company to publish quilt patterns in large quantities and design varieties."

9. **Marie Webster's 1915 book — which contains an alphabetical list of traditional quilt names — is public domain in the USA.**
   URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/24682
   "Public domain in the USA."

10. **Books published before 1964 had to be renewed in their 28th year or enter the public domain — the rule that governs whether McKim (1931) and Hall & Kretsinger (1935) are still protected.**
    URL: https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/renewals.html
    "In the US, books published before 1964 had to get their copyrights renewed at the Library of Congress Copyright Office in their 28th year, or they'd go into the public domain."

11. **Ruby McKim's 101 Patchwork Patterns is the 1931 edition — after the public-domain cutoff, so it is not a public-domain source today.**
    URL: https://101patchworkpatterns.com/index.shtml
    "The information and patchwork patterns on this website are complete and true to the original 1931 edition of 101 Patchwork Patterns by Ruby Short McKim."
