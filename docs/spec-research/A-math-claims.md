# Track A — claims (the twelve the proposals depend on most)

A reviewer should re-open each URL. Access date 2026-10-28.

1. **The quilting seam allowance is a quarter inch, stated as 0.6 cm / 6 mm in metric.**
   https://sewingwithease.com/sewing/seam-allowance-guide/
   "The standard seam allowance is 5/8 inch (1.5 cm) for garment patterns and 1/4 inch (0.6 cm) for quilting."

2. **Plain squares and rectangles are cut half an inch larger than their finished size.**
   https://hcquilts.com/blogs/tips-and-tricks/quilt-block-size-chart
   "For squares and rectangles with straight seams, the formula is simple: Cut size = finished size + 1/2 inch"

3. **Two-at-a-time half-square triangles are cut 7/8 inch larger than the finished unit.**
   https://blog.treasurie.com/half-square-triangles
   "you need to determine what size your finished square needs to be, then add 7/8 inch (2.2cm) to your original square for seam allowances."

4. **Eight-at-a-time (magic 8) half-square triangles are cut at (finished + 7/8) × 2.**
   https://blog.treasurie.com/half-square-triangles
   "Cutting Size = (finished half square triangle + 7/8 inch) x 2"

5. **Quarter-square-triangle (hourglass) units are cut 1 1/4 inch larger than finished.**
   https://www.craftsy.com/post/quarter-square-triangle
   "Here's the formula: Finished block size + 1¼""

6. **The published 4-at-a-time HST cut size is "finished + 1 1/4 inch", but this under-cuts the geometric exact size.**
   https://fabricmathtools.com/hst-size-chart
   "Cut size = finished + 1¼""
   (Geometric exact derived in A-math-findings.md §2: cut = (finished + ½″)·√2.)

7. **No-waste flying geese use one large square cut at finished width + 1¼″ and four small squares cut at finished height + ⅞″.**
   https://weallsew.com/no-waste-flying-geese
   "Take that width measurement (4″) and add 1 ¼″. That will be the size of your large square (5 ¼″.) Take the height measurement (2″) and add ⁷⁄₈″."

8. **One-at-a-time flying geese use a rectangle cut (W+½)×(H+½) and two squares cut H+½″.**
   https://www.quiltersguild.org.uk/includes/files/content/core/pages/B2B_Q149_Flying%20Geese.pdf
   "to make a 2 x 4in (finished) unit you need: • Geese fabric: one 2½ x 4½in rectangle • Background: two 2½in squares"

9. **On-point side setting triangles are cut at (block × 1.414) + 1¼″ and corner triangles at (block ÷ 1.414) + ⅞″.**
   https://www.generations-quilt-patterns.com/setting-triangles.html
   "Finished block size times 1.414" and "Add 1-1/4" to this result for seam allowance" and "Finished block size divided by 1.414" and "Add 7/8" for seam allowances"

10. **Quilting cotton has about 40–42 inches of usable width after trimming selvages.**
    https://stitchinheaven.com/blogs/quilting/how-much-fabric-for-a-quilt
    "Standard 44/45-inch quilting cotton has about 40 to 42 inches of usable width after selvages are trimmed."

11. **Binding strip count is the perimeter (plus an allowance) divided by the usable width.**
    https://www.missouriquiltco.mom/blogs/missouri-star-blog/how-to-figure-yardage-for-quilt-binding
    "Divide the total length of binding needed for your quilt by 40”. This gives you the number of width of fabric strips needed to create your binding."

12. **Longarm backing must be at least 4 inches larger than the quilt top on every side.**
    http://harvestmoonquiltingco.com/longarm-quilting
    "We require that backing be at least four (4”) inches larger on all sides of the quilt top."
